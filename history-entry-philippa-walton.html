Philippa Walton

c. 1674–1749

A pioneering businesswoman whose remarkable life connects the Waltham Abbey gunpowder industry with St Michael & All Angels, Mickleham.

A life remembered inside St Michael & All Angels.

A remarkable woman remembered at Mickleham

Inside St Michael & All Angels, an elaborate eighteenth-century monument invites us to remember Philippa Walton. Its words begin with family and friendship. Beyond them lies the story of a woman who carried responsibility for a household and a substantial industrial business. [1]

Philippa’s story belongs to two places: the mills at Waltham Abbey and the church at Mickleham, where her son became Rector. The surviving memorial brings that wider history into the space visitors can still enter today. [2] [6]

Family, marriage and sudden responsibility

Born Philippa Bourchier around 1674, she married William Walton, a London merchant. The monument names her as the daughter and co-heiress of John Bourchier M.D. It remembers ten children and records that six sons and three daughters survived her. [2] [1]

William died in 1711. Philippa was left with a young family and the management of his business interests. The monument describes her as “a Young & mournfull Widow”, “Charged with the Care of Ten helpless Infants”. These are the family’s words of remembrance, preserved in stone. [2] [1]

Taking charge of the gunpowder mills

Philippa took over an enterprise with a London office and production at Balham and Waltham Abbey. Peace in 1713 brought a fall in demand after the War of the Spanish Succession. She closed the Balham operation and concentrated production at Waltham Abbey. [2]

In January 1719 she bought three more powder mills and rights to the Mill Stream. Her son John joined in 1723 with a quarter share, becoming an equal partner in 1730. Philippa continued to take an active part in the business. [2]

Her achievement was in ownership and management: directing an industrial enterprise through difficult trading conditions. It should not be confused with the physical manufacturing work carried out by the people employed at the mills. [2]

The Walton family and Mickleham

Philippa’s son, Philip Walton, became Rector of Mickleham in 1745. Westminster School’s archive identifies him as the son of William and Philippa and records his appointment as 6 February 1744/5, using the contemporary double-year date. [6]

The British Library’s Hardwicke Papers catalogue also identifies Philip as Rector of Mickleham in records dated 1751–1753. Philippa therefore lived during the early years of her son’s ministry here. Her monument records her death on 7 December 1749. [5] [6] [1]

Her memorial at St Michael’s

The monument remains a historical object in its own right. Historic England identifies it as an “ashlar and marble rococo wall monument”, with shell features and a swan-neck pediment. [4]

Its inscription is also unusually personal. It honours Philippa’s care for her children: she “secured and improved their Property” and “formed their Minds by her Instructions & Example”. These selected words describe both practical responsibility and the family’s affection for her. [1]

The monument spells her name PHILLIPA. The article uses Philippa, as in the Royal Gunpowder Mills history. It records her death “in the 74th Year of her Age”; we retain that wording rather than treating it as an exact modern statement of age. [1] [2]

Read the full memorial inscription

Transcribed from the supplied monument photograph. Historical spelling and capitalisation are retained; long-s letterforms are rendered as s, raised lettering is set on the line, and spacing and punctuation are lightly regularised. This is a reading transcription, not a facsimile.

In grateful Memory of (the best of Wives, Mothers, and Friends) Mrs PHILLIPA WALTON Daughter and Coheiress of JOHN BOURCHIER M.D. and Relict of WILLIAM WALTON late of LONDON Merchant, (A Gentleman worthy of and happy, in her tenderest Affection.) His early Death left Her a Young & mournfull Widow. Charged with the Care of Ten helpless Infants: Of Whom Six Sons and Three Daughters survived, to Comfort the Life and lament the Death of their Excellent and Amiable Parent; Who, after having secured and improved their Property by her Application & Address, and formed their Minds by her Instructions & Example, retired from the Hurry of Active Life, to end her Days in Piety & Peace. She died (in Calm & Christian Serenity) on the 7th day of December 1749, in the 74th Year of her Age, and is Interred near this Place.

An industrial legacy

The Walton family continued at Waltham Abbey after Philippa’s death. The Crown bought the mills in 1787; the site became the Royal Gunpowder Mills. [3]

Modern recognition has brought her industrial achievements to a wider audience. A blue plaque at the Royal Gunpowder Mills honours her as a “Pioneering industrialist and entrepreneur”. The Essex Women’s Commemoration Project lists her among the women it has commemorated. [7] [8]

At Mickleham, her memorial offers another way into that story: a surviving object, a family’s tribute and a connection between parish life and the history of industry. [1]
