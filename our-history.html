Our History

Christian worship has taken place at this site for more than a thousand years. The history of St Michael and All Angels is closely connected with the history of Mickleham itself.

A story across the centuries

A place of worship through time

The parish’s historical account describes a church whose story reaches back more than a thousand years. The details below preserve that account, including where it expresses a historical interpretation.

  1. Early history

    Saxon traces

    The parish history describes the original church as a small Saxon nave and chancel. It says traces of early work remain and suggests some may date to around 950.

  2. 1086

    A church in Domesday

    The Domesday Book entry quoted by the parish records a church and two acres of meadow at Mickleham.

  3. c. 1180

    Norman work

    The old parish history dates much of the present church to the Norman period, around 1180. It points to the chancel arch, lancet windows and font as important historic features.

  4. c. 1300

    The Norbury Chapel

    The side chapel, now called the Norbury Chapel, is described as dating from around 1300. The parish history also records the Wyddowsoun tomb and brasses there.

  5. 1823–1891

    Restoration and change

    The church underwent significant alterations: nave arches and piers were replaced in 1823, later work followed in the nineteenth century, and the north aisle was dismantled in 1891. The parish’s original history reflects on what was changed and what remains.

  6. 1913

    The bells

    The parish account says four of the larger bells completing the peal of six were given in memory of Sir James Lawrence in 1913.

  7. Today

    A living parish

    St Michael and All Angels continues as a place of worship and community life for Mickleham and Westhumble.

Historic features and further reading

The parish account also describes the chancel’s “weeping” alignment, medieval tombstones beside the north door, the Flemish pulpit, carved plaques and six bells. It records a watercolour of the church by J. M. W. Turner held by Tate.

These details are drawn from the previous parish history and have not been independently reassessed during this content migration. For research into Mickleham and Westhumble, the old site points to a guide to the oldest part of the churchyard and to the Mickleham & Westhumble Local History Group.
