Our History

Christian worship has taken place at this site for more than a thousand years. The history of St Michael and All Angels is closely connected with the history of Mickleham itself.

A STORY ACROSS THE CENTURIES

A place of worship through time

Explore key moments in the church’s history, from its earliest traces to parish life today.
Open each period to discover more of the story.

  1. Early history

    Saxon traces

    The original church is thought to have been a small Saxon nave and chancel. Traces of early work remain and may date to around 950.
  2. 1086

    A church in Domesday

    The Domesday Book entry for Mickleham records a church and two acres of meadow.
  3. c. 1180

    Norman work

    Much of the present church dates to the Norman period, around 1180, including the chancel arch, lancet windows and font.
  4. c. 1300

    The Norbury Chapel

    The side chapel, now called the Norbury Chapel, dates from around 1300. Its memorials include the Wyddowsoun tomb and brasses.
  5. 1823–1891

    Restoration and change

    The church underwent significant alterations: nave arches and piers were replaced in 1823, with further changes during the nineteenth century and the dismantling of the north aisle in 1891.
  6. 1913

    The bells

    Four of the larger bells completing the peal of six were given in memory of Sir James Lawrence in 1913.
  7. Today

    A living parish

    St Michael and All Angels continues as a place of worship and community life for Mickleham and Westhumble.
