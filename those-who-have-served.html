William Mowsurth · 1507–1513

Rector · 1507–1513

William Mowsurth

Their story

William Mowfurth was a late-medieval English priest whose ministry connected Mickleham with several Surrey parishes in the years immediately before the Reformation. Historical records identify him as Rector of Puttenham, Mickleham and Streatham. He died in 1513. Mowfurth appears to have held more than one parish living, a practice that was possible among clergy of his period. His connection with Streatham can be traced to at least 1492, when he appears in the historical list of Rectors of St Leonard's Church. His period as Rector of Mickleham is traditionally recorded as 1507–1513, although the precise starting date at Mickleham still requires confirmation from the original ecclesiastical records. Remarkably, a physical memorial to Mowfurth has survived for more than five centuries. His monumental brass is preserved at St Leonard's Church, Streatham. It depicts him as a priest in Mass vestments, with his hands joined in prayer, standing above a Latin memorial inscription. Historic England records the brass among the significant monuments in the chancel of St Leonard's and dates it to 1513. The survival of the brass is particularly valuable because it gives us something much more personal than a name in a list of Rectors: it allows us to see how William Mowfurth chose to be commemorated at the end of his life. The priestly vestments and prayerful pose place him unmistakably within the religious world of England before the Reformation. He died more than twenty years before Henry VIII's break with Rome and therefore ministered to Mickleham when its worship was still part of the medieval Western Church. Mowfurth's memorial was important enough to attract the attention of later antiquarians. The British Library preserves a rubbing of the brass in the collection of Alfred Ridley Bax, catalogued as a rubbing of William Mowfurth, Rector of Streatham and Mickelham, at Streatham, 1513. A second British Library catalogue record also identifies a sepulchral brass of William Mowfurth at Streatham dated 1513. His connection with Mickleham is therefore not based solely upon later local tradition. It is independently recorded by the British Library, while the general index to the Surrey Archaeological Collections describes William Mowfurth specifically as Rector of Puttenham, Mickleham and Streatham and directs readers to a discussion of his brass in volume 32, pages 125–127. An old image preserved at Mickleham records him as “Wm. Mowsurth, Rector; d. 1513”, with a later handwritten annotation, “Rector of Mickleham 1507–1513.” The spelling of his surname varies in historical material, but modern archival catalogues generally use Mowfurth. William Mowfurth therefore provides a rare tangible link with Mickleham's pre-Reformation history. More than five hundred years after his death, his memorial survives at Streatham, historical rubbings of it survive in the British Library, and an old representation preserved at Mickleham continues to connect his story with the parish he once served.

Gerrard Andrewes D.D. · 1800–1813

Rector · 1800–1813

Gerrard Andrewes D.D.

Their story

Gerrard Andrewes (1750–1825) was educated at Westminster and Trinity College, Cambridge. He was ordained deacon in 1776 and priest in 1778.

During his Mickleham incumbency he also became Rector of St James’s Piccadilly in 1802 and Dean of Canterbury in 1809.

Did you know?

He was offered the Bishopric of Chester in 1812 and declined it.

Alfred Burmester · 1813–1867

Rector · 1813–1867

Alfred Burmester

Their story

Alfred Burmester served as Rector of Mickleham for approximately 54 years. He is buried in a vault beneath the chancel with his wife Jane and two sons who died in infancy.

St Michael’s in their time

His ministry coincided with substantial nineteenth-century rebuilding and restoration at St Michael’s.

Did you know?

Woodwork he obtained in Belgium around 1840 was later incorporated into the church pulpit.

Arthur W. Bedford · 1920–1936

Rector · 1920–1936

Arthur W. Bedford

Their story

Local historical material records Sunday services at both Mickleham and Westhumble during Arthur Bedford’s ministry. He was assisted by a curate and retired clergy, including his predecessor William Richard Lloyd.

St Michael’s in their time

In 1929, 27 candidates were presented for confirmation.

Did you know?

A parish account from 1925 describes a water-powered church organ and pressure problems after houses were built in Dell Close.

Parish clergy · 2013–2017

Rev. Malcolm Raby B.Ed.

Their story

Malcolm Raby came to Mickleham in June 2013 as a house-for-duty priest after retiring from full-time ministry as Vicar of Over and Longstanton in the Diocese of Ely.

He lived in the parsonage and undertook parish duties without stipend under the house-for-duty arrangement. Parish material announced his retirement in November 2017.

Canon John Harkin B.A. · 2018–2020

Rector · 2018–2020

Canon John Harkin B.A.

Their story

Canon John Harkin returned to St Michael & All Angels in 2018, around twenty years after his earlier ministry in Mickleham. His return gave the parish an unusual continuity: a priest who already knew the church and its community came back to serve a new generation. He remained at St Michael’s until 2020. This was John Harkin’s second period of ministry at Mickleham. His earlier service, from 1993 to 1998, is recorded separately in this archive.
